Engage

Learning & Teaching | Automated formative assessment and dynamic question design

Project Summary

Automated response evaluation with contextual question generation.

Engage is a digital formative learning tool designed to evaluate student text responses and provide immediate feedback. The platform scores answers from 1 to 10, highlights specific areas for improvement, and supports dynamic question creation aligned with course materials through Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).

The Challenge

Learners often lack opportunities to test their understanding between scheduled classes and formal assessments. Static question banks quickly date, and tutors require practical ways to link formative questions directly to evolving teaching content without increasing manual marking burdens.

Context

Formative engagement declines when students do not receive rapid feedback on their work. Providing detailed individual commentary across large cohorts demands substantial staff time, making automated decision support valuable for preliminary student self-review.

Contribution

  • Concept development
  • Educational design
  • Digital product design
  • Knowledge-base architecture
  • RAG integration
  • Evaluation planning

Key Functions

  • Dynamic question generation indexed to course materials
  • Tutor-managed content knowledge base
  • Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) architecture
  • Automated response analysis with criteria-referenced guidance
  • Scoring rubric from 1 to 10 with developmental recommendations
  • Up to three guided attempts per question to encourage iterative learning
  • Engagement metrics and telemetry tracking

Interface Screenshot

Evaluation & Lessons

Testing examined automated scoring consistency across diverse student responses and verified factual fidelity against uploaded module texts. Grounding questions in specific course documents through Retrieval-Augmented Generation produced far more accurate and pedagogically useful items than generic prompt approaches.

Governance and Oversight

Automated scoring and feedback require suitable oversight and support, rather than replace, educator judgement. Feedback quality depends on supplied materials, question design, and appropriate human moderation.
