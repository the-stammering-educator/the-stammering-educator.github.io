BPP Engage

Learning and Teaching | Automated formative assessment and dynamic question design[cite: 1]

Project Summary

BPP Engage is a digital formative learning tool designed to evaluate student text responses and provide immediate feedback[cite: 1]. The platform scores answers from one to ten, highlights areas for improvement, and supports dynamic question creation aligned with course materials through Retrieval-Augmented Generation[cite: 1].

Challenge

Learners often lack opportunities to test their understanding between scheduled classes and formal assessments[cite: 1]. Static question sets quickly become outdated, and tutors require practical ways to link formative questions directly to changing teaching content without increasing their marking workload[cite: 1].

Context

Formative participation declines when students do not receive rapid feedback on their work[cite: 1]. Supplying detailed individual commentary across large cohorts requires significant time, making automated decision support useful for preliminary student review[cite: 1].

Contribution

My contribution focused on[cite: 1]:

  • Concept development[cite: 1]
  • Educational design[cite: 1]
  • Digital product design[cite: 1]

  • Evaluation planning[cite: 1]

Solution

The system combines automated response scoring, targeted improvement suggestions, and dynamic question generation linked to tutor-managed course documents[cite: 1].

Design Approach

The tool started as a proof of concept evaluating student answers against predefined model answers[cite: 1]. It then expanded to incorporate Retrieval-Augmented Generation[cite: 1]. This addition indexes verified curriculum materials, enabling the tool to create contextual questions and assess student work against specific syllabus criteria[cite: 1].

Key Functions

  • Dynamic question creation[cite: 1]
  • Tutor-managed content knowledge base[cite: 1]
  • Retrieval-Augmented Generation[cite: 1]
  • Automated response analysis[cite: 1]
  • Score from one to ten[cite: 1]
  • Feedback on answer quality with specific improvement points[cite: 1]
  • Up to three attempts per question[cite: 1]
  • Engagement tracking[cite: 1]

Evaluation

Testing examined automated scoring consistency across student answers and verified the factual relevance of dynamically generated questions against uploaded module texts[cite: 1].

Limitations

Automated scoring and feedback require suitable oversight and should support, rather than replace, tutor judgement[cite: 1]. Feedback quality depends on supplied content, question design, and appropriate human oversight[cite: 1]. The tool is an evaluated prototype and does not hold live production status[cite: 1].

Lessons Learned

Grounding dynamic questions in specific course documents through Retrieval-Augmented Generation produced far more useful questions than generic prompts[cite: 1]. Tutors still need clear controls to verify generated items before publishing them to learners[cite: 1].

Next Steps

Further development would depend on user need, evaluation evidence, technical feasibility and available institutional support[cite: 1].
